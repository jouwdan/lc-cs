# Ask questions that can change the decision

Create a table with columns claim, evidence needed, possible failure and mitigation. Ask what “accuracy” measures, how examples were labelled, whether test data was separate, and how performance differs across relevant conditions. Explain why 95% accuracy could hide failure on a rare category. Propose a baseline such as recommending the most frequently borrowed category and compare against it.

Identify data that is necessary and data that is merely tempting to collect. Use fictional records to illustrate risks; do not survey classmates about sensitive preferences. Explain retention, access and correction in ordinary language. Include one accessibility barrier and one environmental cost. Record one argument against your initial view so the evaluation is more than confirmation of a preference.
