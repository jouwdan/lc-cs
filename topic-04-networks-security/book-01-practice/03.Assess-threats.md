# Match threat and control

Choose a protection for each problem.

Use **confidentiality** for who can read data, **integrity** for unauthorised changes, and **availability** for keeping a service usable.

## 1. Study one example

| Threat | Control | How it helps | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Someone accidentally deletes bookings | Tested separate backup | Restores the deleted records | Does not prevent the original deletion |

## 2. Complete three more rows

Choose a control for each problem:

1. A member tries to change another member's booking.
2. A fake message asks for the organiser's password.
3. The network connection fails before a booking is confirmed.

For each, write **what the control does**, not just its name.

## 3. Check your choices

1. **Permissions:** members can change only their own bookings. This protects integrity; an authorised organiser can still make an error.
2. **Verify independently:** contact the organiser through a known channel before responding. This helps detect phishing; do not use contact details supplied only by the suspicious message.
3. **Clear failure message and a checked alternative process:** tell the user that the booking is not confirmed and let them contact the organiser. A backup network could restore access, but adds cost. A data backup alone does not restore the network connection.

Other controls are valid if you explain how they address the specific threat.

**Optional:** explain why a password plus a possession-based token is MFA, but two passwords are not two different factor types.
