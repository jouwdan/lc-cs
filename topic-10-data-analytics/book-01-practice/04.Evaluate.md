# Explain your results

Compare the readings and state a limit.

1. Make a two-row results table: A has four accepted readings, mean 7.00, median 6.00; B has four, mean 3.75, median 4.00.
2. Complete: “In this sample, ___ had the lower median: ___ compared with ___. Each group had ___ accepted readings.”
3. Add one limitation: the dataset is tiny, fictional, and has no time or sampling information. Do not claim anything about actual school areas.
4. Write one useful next measurement for a real investigation, such as readings from both areas at the same times over several days.
5. Check the following before taking the quiz:

- I can explain why all three rows were rejected.
- I kept the valid zero.
- I tested empty, odd, and even lists.
- I can distinguish a lower median in this sample from a universal claim.

**Optional Higher Level task:** sketch `Area` and `Reading` tables. Label the primary keys and the foreign key. Explain how changing an area's name once could update every joined result. You do not need to implement another database to demonstrate that reasoning.

Keep your prediction table and four-sentence conclusion. These are your evidence of learning; a long report is not required.
